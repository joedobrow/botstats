The fantasy formula

A score is a per-game average, so playing more games neither helps nor hurts. Every component below is multiplied by its worth and the results are added up.

Two components that aren't a straight multiplication

Staying alive. Every game starts at {{ "%+g"|format(death_base) }} and each death takes {{ "%g"|format(-death_each) }} off it. {{ (death_base / -death_each)|round|int }} deaths cancel it exactly; past that it goes negative. Dying is the only thing that can take points away.

Game length. Longer games hand out more of almost everything, so a raw score rewards slow games. Across every match on record a game's length predicts its score at {{ "%g"|format(duration_slope) }} points a minute, and that much is taken back out: a player whose games average {{ "%g"|format(duration_mean) }} minutes is unaffected, longer loses, shorter gains.

What isn't counted

Winning, hero, position, who drafted whom, and which abilities were taken. Sentry wards placed score nothing — they're already counted when a sentry kills a ward. Tower kills, first blood and Roshan were dropped: building damage covers the same ground more finely.
